# Seed hull — filling between linked seeds

Brief, prediction and gate: [../briefs/seed-hull.md](../briefs/seed-hull.md), committed before
any run. Code: tag `archive/seed-hull`.

**Question.** Large lesions with many detections lose recall to **inter-frond gaps**, the
concavities between fronds that `P` leaves empty. Does filling the space between seeds whose
heat-map bumps overlap (linked within 2 · `sigmaSeed` = 64 px) raise IoU?

**Prediction (from the brief).** Arm B (triangle fill): ΔIoU +0.005 to +0.015, from recall in
the largest GT-area quintile (+0.02 or more), with the precision guard holding. Arm C
(closing): a smaller ΔIoU than B.

**Protocol.** 68 images (DRAC22 41, TUH 27), cached 5-fold CV `P` through `run_cache`,
whole-image scoring, `tau` 0.5, relax 0.20. Each arm is paired per image against `"none"`,
which reproduces the reference `2026-09-17-relax-0.20.csv` to 5e-16. Percentile bootstrap on
the mean Δ, 10 000 resamples, `rng(20260917)`, 97.5 % CI (Bonferroni over two arms). Exact
two-sided sign test, ties excluded. Precision guard: mean precision over the three smallest
GT-area quintiles may not fall by more than 0.01. Script: a scratch `.m` file, not committed.

## Result: both arms fail. Reject both.

**The prediction is falsified.** B is inert, not wrong. C's mean ΔIoU is positive but
indistinguishable from zero, and it trips the guard. For completeness, the paired B − C
difference is −0.0054, 95 % CI [−0.0196, +0.0079].

### Why B does nothing

Only 193 of 1 291 Delaunay triangles (15 %) have all three edges within 64 px, and **97 % of
their area is already in the mask**. B adds 4 104 px across 68 images, 0.14 % of the mask
area. C adds 17.4 %. Detections land on the vessels the network already responds to. The
inter-frond gaps lie between those vessels but outside the tight seed triangles, so the
seed geometry holds nothing that `P` did not already say. Closing at the same 64 px scale
reaches the gaps because it follows the mask's walls, not the seeds.

| Q5 (50 314–432 533 px) | 14 | **+0.0613** | −0.0287 | +0.1022 |

- **The mechanism holds on large lesions.** On the five images that motivated the brief,
  which were picked by eye and so are description, not evidence: `1115` 0.786 → 0.830,
  `nv (13)` 0.749 → 0.847, `nv (17)` 0.584 → 0.723, `nv (21)` 0.666 → 0.758,
  `nv (27)` 0.538 → 0.789.
- **Everywhere else it is a precision / recall trade that loses.** Q1–Q4 all lose IoU.
  Largest losses: `nv (14)` −0.127, `144` −0.095, `nv (1)` −0.088, `nv (6)` −0.080,
  `194` −0.075.
- **Devices:** DRAC22 +0.0003, 95 % CI [−0.0116, +0.0126]. TUH +0.0136, 95 % CI
  [−0.0147, +0.0445].
- **Folds:** −0.0001, +0.0247, +0.0075, −0.0125, +0.0071.
- The link gate did not confine C to large lesions. Linked seeds occur on lesions of every
  size, so being linked is a poor proxy for "large".

### A correction to the brief

The brief says C "bridges gaps up to 2 · 32 = 64 px". That holds only between *extended*
walls. Two small blobs 55 px apart are not bridged, because a disk of radius 32 fits past
their corners. `test_cnngvf` was written to the correct statement. The arm itself, as
defined in the brief, is unchanged.

## Conclusion

Neither arm is adopted. The seed triangle fill is inert on this `P`: seeds carry no
information about the gaps beyond what `P` already has. A closing at the heat-map scale does
recover inter-frond gaps. It is worth +0.061 IoU on the largest fifth of lesions, but it
costs more than that everywhere else, and it trips the precision guard.

**Follow-up, not tested here.** C's gain is confined to Q5, which suggests a closing gated on
lesion *size* rather than on linkage. Any such gate would be chosen after seeing these
numbers, so it must be pre-registered and cross-fitted, not read off this table. The seed-count
gate k that the brief rejected is one candidate, and this result is the first evidence that
something like it might matter. The underlying cause is model-side: `P` tracks vessels even though
the network trains on FilledGT, which marks the space between them. That points at training
(epochs, loss) rather than post-processing, as [SPEC](../SPEC.md) already argues.
